# Marlowe Kessler LLP — Litigation Calendaring & Deadlines Policy

Fictional sample document for demonstration purposes. Applies to all litigated matters in the Business & Contracts and Family Law groups. Effective April 2026. Owner: Litigation Support Manager (Priya Natarajan).

## 1. Why This Policy Exists

A missed deadline is the most common source of malpractice claims. Every deadline in a litigated matter must be calendared by two people and confirmed by the responsible attorney. No one may rely on memory, email flags, or personal calendars.

## 2. Two-Person Calendaring

- When any document that triggers a deadline arrives (a served complaint, a court order, a scheduling order, discovery requests), the receiving staff member forwards it to docketing@ within 1 hour.
- The docketing clerk enters the deadline in the Clio firm calendar within 4 business hours. A second docketing clerk independently calculates the same deadline. If the two calculations differ, the responsible attorney decides and the reason is noted in the matter.
- Every deadline gets automatic reminders at 14 days, 7 days, 3 days, and 1 day before it falls due, sent to the responsible attorney, the assigned paralegal, and the docketing inbox.

## 3. Common Federal Court Deadlines (reference: Federal Rules of Civil Procedure)

Docketing clerks must confirm every deadline against the current Federal Rules of Civil Procedure and the local rules and the judge's standing orders, which can shorten or extend these periods.

| Event | Typical deadline | FRCP reference |
|---|---|---|
| Answer to a complaint after being served | 21 days after service | Rule 12(a)(1)(A)(i) |
| Answer if the defendant waived service | 60 days after the waiver request was sent (90 days if sent outside the U.S.) | Rule 12(a)(1)(A)(ii) |
| Responses to interrogatories, document requests, and requests for admission | 30 days after being served | Rules 33(b)(2), 34(b)(2)(A), 36(a)(3) |
| Interrogatories per party | No more than 25, including discrete subparts, unless the court allows more | Rule 33(a)(1) |
| Motion for summary judgment | Unless a local rule or order says otherwise, any time until 30 days after the close of all discovery | Rule 56(b) |

Unanswered requests for admission are treated as admitted (Rule 36(a)(3)). These deadlines are flagged red in Clio.

## 4. Counting Days

Clerks count time under FRCP Rule 6(a): exclude the day of the triggering event, count every day including weekends and legal holidays, and include the last day. If the last day falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the period continues to the next day that is not one of those. When service is made by mail, 3 days are added under Rule 6(d).

## 5. Firm Internal Buffer

The firm treats every court deadline as due 2 business days earlier than the actual date. Drafts of any filing must reach the responsible attorney 5 business days before the actual deadline.

## 6. Extensions

- Extension requests to opposing counsel must be confirmed in writing (email is acceptable) and calendared the same day.
- A stipulated extension of a court deadline is not effective until the court approves it if the rules or the judge's order require approval. Until approval, the original deadline stays on the calendar.

## 7. When a Deadline Is At Risk

If a deadline may be missed, the responsible attorney must notify the Managing Partner, Thomas Marlowe, immediately. The firm's malpractice carrier must be notified of any missed deadline within 24 hours of discovery.
