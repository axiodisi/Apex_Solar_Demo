## The Dispute in Brief

Henley and Redstone signed a Warehousing and Fulfillment Services Agreement dated March 1, 2024 (the "Agreement"). Henley alleges Redstone breached the Agreement during the 2025 peak season by (1) missing the 48-hour shipping standard on more than 22% of orders between October and December 2025, and (2) failing to report inventory shrinkage of 1,940 units. Henley terminated the Agreement on January 9, 2026.

The complaint was filed February 10, 2026 and served on Redstone the same week. Redstone filed its answer and a counterclaim on March 5, 2026, claiming $186,400 in unpaid storage and early-termination fees.

## Key Facts

- Section 4.2 of the Agreement requires Redstone to ship 98% of orders within 48 hours of receipt during "Peak Season" (October 1 – December 31).
- Section 9.3 caps Redstone's liability at the fees paid in the prior 12 months unless the breach results from gross negligence or willful misconduct. Fees paid in the 12 months before termination were $264,000. Overcoming the cap is the central legal issue.
- At a June 3, 2025 planning meeting, Redstone's operations manager, Karen Pruitt, was told Henley expected peak-season volume roughly double 2024 levels. Pruitt's deposition (taken September 17, 2026) is key to showing Redstone knew it was understaffed.
